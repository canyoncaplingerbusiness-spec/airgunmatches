# GunMatches.com — what to say, and why

Read this once before sending anything. It's short, and it will stop you making
the pitch that gets you dismissed.

---

## What the research changed

**In practical and precision firearm shooting, you have a well-entrenched
incumbent: PractiScore.** Over 700 clubs and around a hundred thousand
competitors use it every month for registration and scoring, and 99% of NRL22
clubs run on it. USPSA, IDPA, Steel Challenge and PRS clubs live there.

This matters enormously for what you say.

**Do not pitch GunMatches as a replacement for PractiScore.** A match director
who hears that will stop reading. Their registration, payments, squadding and
scoring already work, their shooters already have accounts, and you are a website
they have never heard of.

**Pitch the thing that genuinely doesn't exist: one calendar across every kind of
shooting.** PractiScore is organised around its own sports and requires an
account to browse comfortably. Nothing lists an airgun field target match, an
NRL22 match and a sporting clays shoot on the same page, filterable by state. You
do. That's the gap, and it's real.

For those clubs the ask is small and the answer is easy: *list your match here as
well — it's free, takes two minutes, and puts you in front of shooters who
wouldn't otherwise know you exist.* Nothing changes about how they run the match.

## Where you're strongest

**Airgun competition has no PractiScore.** Schedules are scattered across
AirgunNation threads, Hard Air Magazine forums, club Google Sites and Facebook
posts. That fragmentation is exactly why your 29 events took real effort to
gather — and exactly why a single calendar is obviously useful there.

Airgun is your wedge. Own it completely, then expand. The rankings only exist
because someone finally put the results in one place, and that argument lands
hardest with the people who've been hunting through forum threads for years.

**Live scoring is your differentiator where PractiScore is weakest.** Field
target, benchrest and silhouette aren't well served by practical-shooting tools.
A squad scoring hit-or-miss from a phone with no signal, standings building as the
match runs, results straight into national rankings — that's a real answer to a
real problem, and it's free.

## Three rules for every message

**1. Say it's new.** You have 29 events and a site that launched this month.
Claiming momentum you don't have is the fastest way to lose a small community that
all talks to each other. "New, free, and I'd like your match on it" is a fine
thing to be.

**2. Lead with what's in it for them, not the feature list.** A match director
cares about turnout and about not typing scores into a spreadsheet at 9pm. They do
not care about your architecture.

**3. Never post the same message twice in a forum.** Communities notice
copy-paste instantly. Rewrite it every time, answer people who reply, and be a
member before you're a promoter.

## The order of work

1. **Claim the events you already listed.** Most of your 29 came from public
   sources, and those clubs don't know they're on the site. That's your warmest
   possible first contact — see `outreach-emails.md`, email 1.
2. **The airgun forums**, where your calendar is already useful today.
3. **Firearm clubs**, once at least a handful of rimfire, centerfire or shotgun
   events are on the board so the calendar doesn't look empty to them.

Step 3 before step 2 is the mistake to avoid. A centerfire club that visits today
sees an airgun site.
