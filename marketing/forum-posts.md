# Forum and social posts

Shooting forums are small, long-memoried, and hostile to advertising. Most have a
rule about self-promotion and all of them have members who enforce it whether it's
written down or not. Getting this wrong once is expensive.

---

## The rules that actually matter

**Read the self-promotion rule before you post anywhere.** Some forums require you
to ask a moderator first. Ask. A moderator who said yes will defend you later.

**Post as a person, not a brand.** You built it, you shoot, you got tired of
hunting through threads for match dates. That's the story, and it's true.

**Never paste the same text twice.** Rewrite every time. Communities overlap more
than you'd think and copy-paste is spotted instantly.

**Answer everyone who replies, including the sceptics** — especially them, and
especially in public. How you handle the first "what's your angle?" sets your
reputation more than the post did.

**Then stay.** Post the thread, answer questions, and keep showing up afterwards.
A stranger who posts a link and vanishes is a spammer. Someone still answering
questions in three weeks is a member.

---

## Where to go, in order

**1. The airgun forums** — AirgunNation and the Hard Air Magazine community.
This is where most of your 29 events came from, and where the calendar is
obviously useful today. Start here.

**2. Discipline-specific Facebook groups** — field target, benchrest, NRL22,
silhouette. Post to the group, not to a page nobody follows.

**3. Reddit** — r/airguns, and the discipline subreddits once you have firearm
events. Reddit is the harshest of the three about promotion. Be a participant
first; a two-day-old account posting a link gets removed on sight.

**4. Your own clubs' Facebook pages**, once a few directors are on side. A club
sharing it carries far more weight than you posting it.

## Post 1 — airgun forum, introduction

Where your listings came from. Owning that up front takes away the only real
objection.

> **Title:** I built a national airgun match calendar — and your club's matches
> might already be on it
>
> I got fed up trying to work out what matches were on this season. The
> information is out there, but it's spread across threads here, club Google
> Sites, Facebook posts and a few PDFs, and half of it is last year's.
>
> So I put it in one place: gunmatches.com
>
> It's 29 matches at the moment across field target, benchrest, silhouette,
> precision rifle and long range, filterable by state. Most of them I found on
> this forum, which I should be upfront about — if your match is listed and you'd
> rather it wasn't, tell me and it's gone today. If it's listed and something's
> wrong, tell me and I'll fix it.
>
> Two things beyond the calendar, both free:
>
> Match directors can post results afterwards, straight out of a spreadsheet.
> Those build a national ranking by finishing place — so a shooter's season shows
> up across every club they shot at, which as far as I know hasn't existed for
> airgun before.
>
> And there's live scoring if you want it. Squad scorers tap hit or miss on their
> phone, standings build as the match runs, and it works with no signal — it saves
> everything on the phone and sends it when you're back in range. That last part
> was the whole reason I built it rather than using something off the shelf.
>
> No account needed, no ads, nothing to pay, and I'm not selling anything. Happy
> to answer questions or take criticism.

## Post 2 — shorter, for a Facebook group

Groups scroll fast. Long posts don't get read.

> Anyone else lose track of what matches are on?
>
> I built a calendar for it: gunmatches.com — every match I could find, sorted by
> date, filterable by state and by what it's shot with.
>
> Free, no account. Match directors can post results and they feed a national
> ranking.
>
> Most of what's on there now is airgun. If you run a match and want it listed,
> or your match is already up and something's wrong, let me know and I'll sort it.

## Post 3 — after a match uses live scoring

**The one that will actually work.** Wait until a real match has run on it, then
post the standings. Proof beats description, and one director's word is worth more
than everything you say about yourself.

> **Title:** Live scoring at [club] this weekend — how it went
>
> [Club] ran their [match] on the live scoring at gunmatches.com on Saturday.
> [Number] competitors across [number] squads, scored from phones.
>
> Standings as they finished: [link]
>
> A few things worth reporting honestly:
>
> [what worked]
>
> [what didn't — say it. Every match director reading this has been sold
> something that worked perfectly in the demonstration.]
>
> The offline part did what it was supposed to. [Detail — signal dropped, taps
> kept saving, everything synced when it came back.]
>
> It's free and there's no account. If you run matches and want to try it,
> gunmatches.com/organizers.html explains the setup, or ask me here.

## Post 4 — Reddit

Reddit punishes anything that smells like marketing. No bold text, no feature
list, no link in the title.

> **Title:** Made a site that lists airgun matches by state because I couldn't
> find them anywhere
>
> Every club posts their schedule somewhere different — forum thread, club site,
> Facebook, sometimes a PDF from 2023. I wanted one page.
>
> gunmatches.com. About 30 matches, filter by state and discipline. Free, no
> signup, no ads, and I'm not making money on it — it costs me a domain
> registration.
>
> Match directors can post results and there's a ranking built from finishing
> places. There's also live scoring from a phone that works without signal, which
> is the part I actually needed and couldn't find anywhere else.
>
> Mostly airgun right now, expanding to rimfire and centerfire. Tell me what's
> broken.

## What to do when someone's hostile

Someone will say it's a data grab, or ask what you're really after. Answer
plainly, once, in public:

> Fair question. It's free because it costs me almost nothing to run — it's on
> free hosting and a domain that's about eleven dollars a year. There are no ads
> and I don't sell anything. Submitter emails are never published and never
> shared. If it ever needed money to keep running I'd say so out loud rather than
> quietly changing what it does.

Then stop. Don't argue past the first answer. Everyone else reading has already
made up their mind about which of you is being reasonable.
